Perfect/Normative Rhymes

Perfect rhyme searches result in words that rhyme perfectly with the search word.  If, for example, you search for perfect rhymes for the word "follow", the results will include the words "swallow", "wallow", and "apollo", among others.

 

Perfect rhymes (also called Normative rhymes) are words that have the same sounds beginning at the end of the word, and working back to the vowel sound in the closest syllable that has the lexical stress emphasis.  For example, in the word "follow", all perfect rhymes must match the sounds beginning with the vowel sound of the first syllable, because this is the syllable which has the lexical stress.  On the other hand, in the word "conclude", perfect rhymes must only match the sounds beginning with the vowel sound of the final syllable, because this is the syllable which has the lexical stress.

 

Note: A "perfect rhyme" and a "final syllable rhyme" are the same if the lexical emphasis is on the final syllable of the word. For example, the word "revolt" has the emphasis on the final syllable "volt".  Therefore, only this syllable is used in a perefect rhyme. However, in the word "myopic" has its lexical emphasis on the second-to-last syllable, "op". Therefore, both of the last two syllables are used in the perfect rhyme.
